# National 2047 Strategy

_Generated by `src/strategy.py` on 2026-11-15 from Phase 2-3 outputs._

> **Scope.** Domestic **conventional** generation monitored by CEA (coal, lignite, gas, diesel, nuclear, large hydro). Solar, wind, small hydro and biomass generation are **not quantified** (no authorised actual-generation data), so totals are **not** India's total electricity production or requirement. Generation location is not consumption. Bhutan import is separate.

## 1. Where conventional generation stands
- **FY2025-26 actual:** 1,530 TWh (-2.5% YoY, the first decline since FY2020-21).
- **Growth:** CAGR FY2019-20->FY2025-26 was 3.49%/yr.
- **FY2026-27 statistical forecast (reconciled bottom-up ETS):** 1,674 TWh (95% range 1,619-1,761), of which 6 months are actual. The unreconciled national ETS gives 1,656 TWh (comparison only).
- **FY2027-28:** 1,696 TWh (95% range 1,587-1,880); it includes months beyond the validated 12-month horizon.

## 3. Strategic reading (evidence-based)
1. **Plan around a range, not a point.** The low-high spread in 2047 (1,564-3,690 TWh) exceeds the entire FY2025-26 output. Investment in conventional plants should be staged and reversible.
2. **The FY2025-26 dip and the +7.5% April-September 2026 rebound** show that annual direction is not yet settled. Re-estimate the scenario starting rates each year as new FY actuals arrive.
3. **Coal/lignite is ~84% of conventional output.** Every scenario's growth falls mainly on coal unless a policy mix (`energy_mix_strategy.md`) is adopted.
4. **Renewable generation is the missing half of the strategy.** The total requirement cannot be stated until an authorised actual-generation series for solar and wind is available. The framework is in `final_methodology.md`.

## 4. Limitations
The history is short (87 months, 7 FYs), the extrapolation runs 21 years, the terminal growth rates are assumptions, and there is no renewable or consumption data (by rule). Details: `forecast_uncertainty.md`, `forecasting_assumptions.md` (Phase 3).
