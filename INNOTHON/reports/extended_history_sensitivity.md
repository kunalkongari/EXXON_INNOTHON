# Extended-History Sensitivity

_Generated by `src/make_reports.py` on 2026-11-15._

> **Scope.** All results refer to **domestic conventional generation monitored by CEA** (coal, lignite, gas, diesel, nuclear, large hydro). Solar, wind, small hydro and biomass are excluded (no authorised generation data); the figures therefore do **not** represent total Indian electricity production. Bhutan import is modelled separately.

## Setup
- **Extended national series:** 2018-07..2026-09. The months 2018-07..2019-06 are flagged; they come from the published 'same month previous year' column (#5). The rest is the main data. File: `outputs/data/model_ready_extended_national.csv`. It is never merged into the main panel.
- **Reconciliation issue:** col #5 differs from the originally published col #4 by up to 0.10% (375 overlapping series-months, median 0.000%). This is **unresolved**, so the extended data are **not** used for the main results.
- **Comparability:** same origins (2023-06..2026-08), horizons and metrics as experiment A, with the COVID regressor included. The MASE scale is the main-window seasonal-naive scale, so both runs share identical denominators.
- **ML caveat:** in EXT, RF and XGBoost are trained on the single national series, because no pooled panel exists for the extended period. Their results are not comparable with A and are shown for completeness only.

## Does model selection change?
- Best statistical model on the main data: **ets_auto** (MASE 0.710). With extended data: **lintrend_season** (MASE 0.644).
- **Yes, selection changes.** Trend-type models (linear trend + month dummies, SARIMA) gain most from the extra 12 months, because their trend estimate rests on few seasonal cycles. ETS and Holt-Winters change little.
- **Interpretation:** the national choice between ETS and trend regression is sensitive to history length. That is evidence the short sample limits model identification, and this uncertainty is carried into the scenario ranges.

## Do projected trends change?
- **CAGR:** FY2019-20->FY2025-26 = 3.49%/yr; including the flagged FY2018-19, FY2018-19->FY2025-26 = 2.98%/yr. The FY2018-19 total (1,244,930 GWh) is almost identical to FY2019-20 (1,244,989 GWh).
- Using the extended window would lower the central scenario's starting growth from 3.49% to 2.98%. That change is well inside the low-high scenario range.

## Recommendation
Keep the main dataset as the primary basis. Adopt the extended history only if the 0.10% restatement differences are explained and you explicitly approve. If it is adopted, re-run national selection (the linear trend + month dummies model would then be selected over ETS).
